## Summary

The yellow zúngaro is a large predatory catfish of the Amazon basin. It mainly inhabits river channels and undertakes medium-distance migrations associated with its feeding and reproductive cycles. Historically it was considered a taboo species in Amazonian gastronomy, although its commercial use increased with the development of refrigeration plants that began incorporating it into the fishery market.

## Distribution

Potential yellow zúngaro production is estimated at 1,300 tonnes. The Brazil–Peru–Colombia Border region concentrates 53% of catches, followed by the Peruvian Amazon (23%), the Central Amazon (13%) and the Lower Amazon (7%). It undertakes medium-distance migrations between 100 and 1,000 km

## Uses, management and conservation

It is a species harvested by Amazonian commercial fishing and classified as second commercial class. Its market extends beyond the Amazon, reaching national distribution. Traditionally it had limited acceptance in some Amazonian regions due to cultural taboos associated with its consumption, a situation that has gradually changed with the strengthening of commercial and refrigeration chains.
