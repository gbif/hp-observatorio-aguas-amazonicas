## Common names

- Bolivia: Pacu
- Brazil: Ruelo, Tambaqui, Bocó
- Colombia: Cachama-negra, Gambitana, Gamitana
- Ecuador: Paco
- Peru: Gamitana

## Summary

The gamitana is a medium-sized Amazonian fish with frugivorous habits, whose production is concentrated mainly in the Central Amazon. It migrates during the rising-water season to reproduce and uses flooded forests as habitat during high waters. It is a high-value commercial fishery resource that faces pressures from overfishing, especially on young individuals.

## Distribution

Potential gamitana production is estimated at 15,500 tonnes, concentrated mainly in the Central Amazon (91%). It is also recorded in the Peruvian Amazon, Madeira, Lower Amazon, Brazil-Peru-Colombia Border and Upper Madeira.

## Threat status

Near Threatened (NT) IUCN

## Reproduction

The gamitana begins its migration at the start of the rising-water season and moves upstream to spawn in river channels.

## Feeding

It is a frugivorous fish. Juveniles feed initially on zooplankton and, during the high-water season, progressively incorporate fruits and seeds. Adults feed mainly on these resources.

## Habitat

During the low-water season, adults remain in river channels. As water levels rise, they migrate to flooded forests of different river types, where they find fruits and seeds.

## Uses, management and conservation

The gamitana is a first-class fish, with a regional market within the Amazon. Large individuals fetch high prices and have been subject to heavy exploitation. Currently, juveniles account for a large share of catches and there is a history of overexploitation in the Central Amazon and the Lower Amazon.
